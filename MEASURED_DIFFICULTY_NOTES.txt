How it decides
- Every student has an ability and every question a difficulty on one scale; P(right) = sigmoid(ability - difficulty). Fitting both together
  means a hard question that only strong students got is NOT mistaken for an easy one (plain percent-correct makes that mistake).
- The label is for the question's CLASS LEVEL: the expected score of an average student of that form. >= 72% easy, < 42% hard, else medium.
- Needs at least 8 answers to a question, and a subject needs 100 answers from 15 students. Until then the question keeps the teacher's level.
- Questions with too few answers can get a provisional label predicted from their wording, but only once 40+ questions are measured AND the
  predictor beats "guess the average" in cross-validation. Otherwise they stay unlabelled. These show "(estimated from wording)".
- A student's FIRST answer counts (exam first attempt, else first practice answer), so retakes and repeats do not make a question look easy.
- Never changes a mark, a result, or the difficulty a teacher typed.

Measured on simulated schools (4 forms, 180 questions, 8,400 answers), against the TRUE difficulty:
  strong students given the hard questions: correlation with truth 0.93 (plain percent-correct 0.88); label right 82.8% (plain 78.9%)
  questions given at random:                correlation 0.95; label right 86.1%
Real exams are noisier than the simulation, so expect somewhat lower on your data; run --dry-run once real answers exist.
